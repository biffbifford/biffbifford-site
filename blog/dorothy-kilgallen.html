Dorothy Kilgallen: The Panelist Who Interviewed Jack Ruby

By Biff Bifford · November 25, 2026

Millions of Americans knew Dorothy Kilgallen as the sharp, competitive woman on the right end of the What's My Line? panel. Fewer remember that she was also a serious newspaper reporter, and that she was in the courtroom when Jack Ruby went on trial.

A reporter first

Dorothy Kilgallen was born in Chicago on July 3, 1913, the daughter of a newspaper reporter. She went into the business herself. In 1936 she took part in a newspaper race around the world, traveling only on commercial routes, and the coverage made her famous.

From 1938 she wrote the Broadway column "Voice of Broadway," which spread to dozens of newspapers. In 1945 she and her husband, actor and producer Dick Kollmar, started a radio show called Breakfast with Dorothy and Dick. Beginning in 1950 she was a regular on What's My Line?, where viewers knew her for wanting to win.

She also covered big trials. In 1954 she reported on the Sam Sheppard murder trial in Ohio.

The Jack Ruby trial

In early 1964, Kilgallen went to Dallas for Jack Ruby's trial. Ruby had shot Lee Harvey Oswald, the man accused of killing President Kennedy. By several accounts she got a private interview with Ruby through one of his lawyers. Author Mark Shaw, who wrote a book arguing she was murdered, says she was the only reporter among roughly 400 at the trial to get one.

She later said she had information that would "bust the whole thing open." She died before she published it.

What is documented about her death

  • She died in New York City in November 1965. She was 52.
  • She was found the morning of November 8. Accounts differ on whether a maid or her hairdresser found her.
  • The medical examiner attributed her death to alcohol combined with barbiturates. He said it was undetermined whether it was an accident or something else. Her family considered it an accident.

What people claim

Some writers say she was murdered to stop her from publishing what she knew about the Kennedy assassination. Their points include the following:

  • She was found in a bedroom she did not usually sleep in.
  • Her reading glasses were reportedly missing.
  • Her notes on the Ruby interview and the case have never been found.

Her biographer Lee Israel has said she may have been murdered. But no proof of murder has ever been found. The official finding stands, and it was never changed.

Why her story lasts

Whatever happened, Dorothy Kilgallen was a real working journalist who sat across from one of the most talked-about men in America. Her career is well documented. Her death is a mystery that people will probably keep arguing about.
