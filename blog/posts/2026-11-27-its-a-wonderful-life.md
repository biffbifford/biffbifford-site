Ask people to name a Christmas movie, and *It's a Wonderful Life* comes up fast. But when it opened in December 1946, it was a disappointment.

## A big picture that did not pay off

Frank Capra directed it, with Jimmy Stewart as George Bailey and Donna Reed as Mary. Reports put the cost at about $3.7 million, a lot of money for the time. It reportedly earned about $3.3 million, so it lost money. Critics were lukewarm.

It did earn five Academy Award nominations, including Best Picture. But *The Best Years of Our Lives* won that year. The only award *It's a Wonderful Life* took home was a technical one, for the fake snow.

## The snow

Earlier movies used untoasted cornflakes for snow, which were noisy on the soundtrack, and sometimes asbestos. For this film the studio's special effects team came up with a new mix of water, soap flakes, foamite and sugar. Versions of it have been used in movies since.

## Who else could have been George Bailey

Stewart got the part, and it became the role people think of first when they hear his name. By some accounts, Cary Grant and Henry Fonda were also considered. Fonda and Stewart had once shared an apartment in New York as young actors.

## A mistake that changed everything

In 1974, the movie's copyright ran out. A clerical error meant the owners never filed the renewal paperwork, and the film fell into the public domain.

That meant television stations could show it without paying anything. Many did, every December, for about twenty years. A new generation grew up watching it on TV at Christmas.

Capra, who by then was an old man, said the film's revival astonished him. He said he had not really thought of it as a Christmas story when he started.

By the early 1990s the owners had found a way to take back control of the film, and today it is shown on a fixed schedule. But the habit stuck.

## Why it lasts

The story is a simple one. A man gives up his own dreams to help the people of his town. On the worst night of his life, he is shown what the town would have been like without him. It reminds people that an ordinary life can matter a great deal.
