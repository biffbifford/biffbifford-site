The best time is 9:30 am. Fifteen posts went up between 9:30 and 10:00. A typical one got about 6,900 views, and 6 of the 15 cleared 10,000. Same shape as the posts that got likes, not the quizzes that got views. A show this audience already knows. One person. A turn in the first line. The whole story in that one post. ### 1. Tuesday, November 10, 9:30 am — The Carol Burnett Show

In the “Went with the Wind!” sketch, Scarlett comes down the stairs in a dress made from the curtains, with the curtain rod still through the shoulders. Bob Mackie built the rod into the costume and kept it from the rest of the cast, so the laugh would be real. Harvey Korman cannot hold it together. Carol lands the line about seeing it in the window and not being able to resist it.

This is the Tim Conway post in a gown. A show people can quote, a joke that was a real surprise in the room, and a person at the center. Open on the rod, not on a history of the show. Carol Burnett has only appeared here as one title in a lineup poll, plus the ear-tug story. This moment has not been posted.

### 2. Friday, November 13, 9:30 am — All in the Family

On February 19, 1972, Sammy Davis Jr. played himself. He had left a briefcase in Archie Bunker’s cab and came to the house on Hauser Street to get it back. Archie wanted a picture. On the count of three, Sammy kissed him on the cheek and left. It became one of the biggest laughs the show ever got. The director, John Rich, later said he added the kiss because the written ending felt weak. The writer, Bill Dana, said the kiss was already in his draft.

This was suggested last time and never posted. It is the Hogan’s Heroes shape: a comedy moment that is also about something true, on a show this audience can quote. Friday is the best day for a normal post (18 posts, typical about 4,500 views). Open on the kiss, then the briefcase. Do not turn it into a poll. The lineup poll this week got replies, not likes.

### 3. Sunday, November 15, 9:30 am — M\*A\*S\*H

McLean Stevenson was leaving, so the episode sends Colonel Blake home. The cast filmed the goodbye party on that story. The last page stayed back. Just before the final scene, the others learned that Radar would walk in and say Blake’s plane had been shot down, with no survivors. They filmed it on that first look. Stevenson was still on the set and watched his own character die. The season wrap party was called off.

This was also suggested last time, for a Sunday morning, and it is still unused. The two M\*A\*S\*H posts that did go up, on October 29 and November 1, both cleared 11,000 views with like rates over 2%. The audience wants this show. This is the Don Knotts post in another uniform: a friend leaving, and a last scene that was real for the people in the room. Open on Radar in the doorway. Sunday at 9:30 am, and make it the only story that day.
