## 5. Three posts for next week

Same shape as the posts that got both views and likes: a show this audience already knows, a first line with a turn in it, and a person at the center. Moorehead, Coogan, and Thursday's M\*A\*S\*H post are the models. Darrin is the warning. A trick in the first line can travel a long way and still earn almost no likes.

Two of these stories have been waiting since the October 19 report. They were never posted. Each one should be the only post that day. Fold any extra fact into the post. Skip the second tweet, the bare link, and the poll.

### 1. Friday, November 6, around 9:30 am Pacific — All in the Family

On February 19, 1972, Sammy Davis Jr. played himself. He had left a briefcase in Archie Bunker's cab. He came to the house on Hauser Street to get it back. Archie wanted a picture. On the count of three, Sammy kissed him on the cheek and left. It became one of the biggest laughs the show ever got. The director, John Rich, later said he added the kiss because the written ending felt weak. The writer, Bill Dana, said the kiss was already in his draft.

A comedy moment that is also about something true, on a show this audience can quote. Friday has the highest typical post in the file, about 4,900 views, based on 13 posts. This Friday at 9:30, Darrin got 20,533 views and a 0.50% like rate, because the first line was a trick. Open on the kiss, then the briefcase. The feeling goes in the first line.

### 2. Sunday, November 8, around 9:30 am Pacific — M\*A\*S\*H

McLean Stevenson was leaving, so Colonel Blake spent the episode going home. The cast filmed the goodbye party on that story. Larry Gelbart kept the last page in his desk. Alan Alda was the only actor who knew. The operating-room scene was shot last. Just before it, the others learned that Radar would walk in and say Blake's plane had been shot down over the Sea of Japan, with no survivors. They filmed it on that first look at the page. Stevenson was still on the set and watched his own character die. The season wrap party was called off.

This is Thursday's M\*A\*S\*H post with a broken heart in it. A show people love, a friend leaving, and a last scene that was real for the people in the room. Sunday has the most views of any day in the file: 192,869, from 19 posts. Use 9:30 am, the hour with 18 posts behind it and a typical post at about 6,700 views. This Sunday's Radar post is a different story, and by next Sunday it will be a week old. Open on Radar in the doorway. Do not add a second tweet.

### 3. Tuesday, November 3, around 9:30 am Pacific — I Love Lucy

They would not let Lucille Ball say the word pregnant.

In the second season she was carrying Desi Arnaz Jr., and they wrote it into the show. CBS would not allow "pregnant" on television. The scripts said "expecting." The episode where Lucy has the baby, "Lucy Goes to the Hospital," aired on January 19, 1953. Desi Jr. was born that same morning. The episode drew one of the biggest audiences television had ever had.

A household show, a word they were not allowed to say, and a real baby arriving on the morning the country watched the fake one. That is the Moorehead shape: a face everyone knows, and a fact that lands in the chest. The archive already has the story of Lucy and Desi owning the show, and the story of Lucy saving Star Trek. It does not have this one. Lead with the word they banned, then the birth. Tuesday at 9:30 is the same clock as Karloff this week (6,791 views). Use Lucy. Make it the only post that day.
