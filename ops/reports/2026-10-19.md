This file does not include replies, so there is no way to see whether people were answered. The profile’s post counter still rose by 14, 3, 21, and 16 on the October 16–19 readings. **Sunday between 7 and 10 am, and other days between 6 and 9 am.** Partly tried, and the full stories in that neighborhood worked.

The morning slot worked when the post was a full story. Other posts those three days still went up in the afternoon and evening.

**A show people already love, with a first line that leaves them warm or stunned. Skip the pay-fight story.** Tried, and it worked for views and likes. The advice still matches the numbers. It was not followed, so it was not given a chance to change the follower count.

**The three stories: M\*A\*S\*H on Sunday, October 19; Bewitched on Tuesday, October 21; All in the Family on Thursday, October 23.** Not tried. None of them are in the archive. The M\*A\*S\*H one was for this morning, and it has not gone up.

## 5. Three posts for next week

Same shape as the posts that got both views and likes this week: a show this audience already knows, a first line with a turn in it, and a person at the center. These are the three stories from the last report. They were never posted. The clock time is moved to the window that just did best, about 9:00 to 9:30 am. Make each one the only post that day.

### 1. Tuesday, October 20, around 9:30 am Pacific — Bewitched

In 1959, on the Gary Cooper film *They Came to Cordura*, Dick York and Cooper were pumping a railroad handcar. The director yelled cut. An extra grabbed the handle York was lifting. York said he was suddenly lifting the man’s whole weight, about 180 pounds, and the muscles along the right side of his back tore. He kept working. A decade later he was Darrin on Bewitched, in and out of pain, and in February 1969 he collapsed on the set and was taken to the hospital. He left the show that spring. He called it the worst day of his life, because it was the one job he did not finish. Dick Sargent took over, and the show never stopped to explain where Darrin had gone.

Household show, a hidden injury, a man who felt he had let people down. That is the Festus shape: a face everyone knows, and a life underneath it. Lead with the handcar, then the collapse.

### 2. Friday, October 23, around 9:30 am Pacific — All in the Family

On February 19, 1972, Sammy Davis Jr. played himself. He had left a briefcase in Archie Bunker’s cab. He came to the house on Hauser Street to get it back. Archie wanted a picture. On the count of three, Sammy kissed him on the cheek and left. It became one of the biggest laughs the show ever got. The director, John Rich, later said he added the kiss because the written ending felt weak. The writer, Bill Dana, said the kiss was already in his draft.

A comedy moment that is also about something true, on a show this audience can quote. Friday morning is the next-best day in the file, based on 7 posts. The old plan put this on a Thursday afternoon. Use Friday at 9:30 am instead. Open on the kiss, then the briefcase.

### 3. Sunday, October 25, around 9:00 am Pacific — M\*A\*S\*H

McLean Stevenson was leaving, so Colonel Blake spent the episode going home. The cast filmed the goodbye party on that story. Larry Gelbart kept the last page in his desk. Alan Alda was the only actor who knew. The operating-room scene was shot last. Just before it, the others learned that Radar would walk in and say Blake’s plane had been shot down over the Sea of Japan, with no survivors. They filmed it on that first look at the page. Stevenson was still on the set and watched his own character die. The season wrap party was called off.

This is the Hoss post in another uniform. A show people love, a friend leaving, and a last scene that was real for the people in the room. Sunday between 7:30 and 10:30 am is the slot with the most evidence behind it. Use 9:00 am, the same clock time as Hoss. Open on Radar in the doorway.
