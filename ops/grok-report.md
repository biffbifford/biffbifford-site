Week ending November 20, 2026. The goal is more followers. Views matter only when they come with likes and reposts, because those are the people who might stay.

A fan, in this report, is a like or a repost. Like rate is likes divided by views. The pinned "📌 Welcome! Start here" post from October 28 is left out of every like-rate number below. When last counted it had 1,921 views, 9 likes and 0 reposts. It is not ranked against other posts.

The archive has 238 original posts from October 3 through November 20. Nine posts from the last two days (since Wednesday, November 18, noon Pacific) are still collecting views. They are listed on their own at the bottom and left out of the rankings. The rankings use the 228 finished posts. Follower dates are the day the stats were collected, not the day a post went up.

## 3. Why followers keep falling, and one change

| Date | Followers |

The count is down 837 from the peak and 355 since political posts stopped on October 22. Some followers who came for politics were expected to leave, and that explains part of the loss. It does not explain all of it, because the slide from September 18 to October 22 was already about 14 a day.

**The slide has slowed, but it has not stopped.** From November 3 to November 10 the account lost 137 followers, about 19 a day. From November 10 to November 20 it lost 53, about 5 a day. Between November 10 and November 15 the count was almost flat (down 10, with one day up 15). From November 15 to November 19 it fell 45, about 11 a day, then rose 2 on November 20. The follow reply was already in use throughout, and the improvement since November 10 is the first sign it may be helping. It is too early to call it a win.

**Why big views are not turning into followers.** The posts with the most views are being shown to people who do not follow the account, and they like the post and leave. The Gene Wilder post (91,272 views, 59 reposts) is the clearest case. Meanwhile, the Mel Brooks post (below) earned 296 reposts on 19,046 views. Reposts put a post in front of people who might follow, and views alone do not.

**One change:** make the follow reply give a reason to come back. Right now it says "One true story like this every day. Follow so you don't miss tomorrow's!" Replace it with a one-line teaser for tomorrow's actual story, for example "Tomorrow: the actor who turned down James Bond. Follow so you don't miss it." People follow for something specific more often than for a general promise. Use it as the first reply under the noon story only, and post it within five minutes so it sits near the top. Keep everything else the same for seven days and judge it by the follower readings alone.
